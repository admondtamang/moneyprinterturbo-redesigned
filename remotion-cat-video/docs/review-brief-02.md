# Review brief — Film 02 (narrated cut)

The director's ask: the film must **get attention**, be **reliable**, and be **watchable**.
Those are three different axes with three different failure modes, so they get three reviewers.

## What is being reviewed
`out/thecatcourt-02.mp4` — 1080x1920, 60fps, 35.2s, narrated (Sulafat), scored at 75 BPM.
Same picture as Film 01 slowed to 0.625x via `<Sequence playbackRate>`.

## Panel
| Reviewer | Owns | Fails the film if |
|---|---|---|
| Retention | The first 3 seconds, pacing, drop-off risk | Nothing stops a thumb before 2s; any stretch reads as dead air at the slower tempo |
| Reliability | Every on-screen and spoken claim vs its source | A claim overstates its source, or the narration asserts a cause the evidence does not support |
| Accessibility | Sound-off viewing, legibility, contrast, mix | Narration carries meaning that a muted viewer cannot get; speech is masked by the bed |

## The known gap, stated up front
Film 02 adds a voice track but **no captions for it**. A large share of short-form is watched
muted. Any meaning that exists only in the narration is invisible to those viewers. The on-screen
type carries the headlines, so the film is not meaningless muted — but the narration's
connective tissue is lost. This is the Accessibility reviewer's first question, not an
afterthought.

## Standing constraints the reviewers may NOT override
- **Film 01 (`TheCatCourt01`, `thecatcourt-01.mp4`) is shipped and must not change.** Verified
  pixel-identical through the Remotion upgrade; keep it that way.
- The fact position is load-bearing: **no peer-reviewed study explains why cats push objects off
  surfaces.** The acquittal rests on absent proven intent. Six folklore claims were cut during
  fact-checking and must not return.
- `docs/motion-rules.md` banned-defaults list still applies.
- The loop must stay seamless.
